# Timeslip Ramen · show bible

## The premise

A tiny ramen shop on a rainy street corner in present-day Tokyo. Its door opens onto any century. Most nights
the regulars are the only customers; then the door rattles, the lantern's eye goes wide, and someone from
Japanese history or folklore steps in out of the rain. They get a bowl, a little help with whatever they
came in carrying, and a square shikishi board to sign for the wall. The wall has 35 slots.

The tone is cozy, funny and a little wistful: a mild Ghibli warmth, never a lecture. **Nobody explains
anything in dialogue.** Words and facts go on the cards on top of the picture (word cards in indigo, 豆知識
fact cards on paper), and every fact's sources go into the video description.

## The regulars

| | | Season 1 arc |
|---|---|---|
| **Haru** (ハル) | The cook. Took over the shop from her grandfather, whose note is still pinned to the kitchen hood (雨の夜は誰にでも, "rainy nights: feed whoever") and whose umbrella still stands in the stand. Unflappable: "Irasshaimase" to a thunder god. | Learns she doesn't have to run the shop alone: by the finale Gen-san is officially on bowls. |
| **Kon** (コン) | A fox, 99 years old, who has tried every night for decades to take human form and only ever manages the ears. Eats kitsune udon, counts down the days. | Turns 100 on Fri 11 Dec (the finale airs that day). Seimei tells him a tail comes from being a good fox, not from pretending. His grandmother comes; he gets his second tail and a human face, and decides he likes his ears. |
| **Mika** (ミカ) | 18, final year of high school, studying for January's entrance exams at the end of the counter. Asks every visitor something for her notebook ("people, not dates"). | Gets a D on a mock exam (Ep. 4), essay advice from two rival writers (Ep. 8), a plum branch from the god of learning (Ep. 10), a nightmare eaten (Ep. 11), and an A in history (Ep. 12). |
| **Gen-san** (源さん) | The oldest regular. Drove a taxi past Shibuya Station for 42 years. Calls everything "cosplay". | Softens over Hachikō (Ep. 5), finally believes when the thunder god asks for his belly button (Ep. 6), sees the house spirit, and decides at Inō Tadataka's example that he isn't too old to start something (Ep. 7): he starts helping Haru. |
| **Chō** (提灯) | The paper lantern by the door, with one eye. Goes wide-eyed whenever the door is about to open; winks at the end of every episode. | Says her first word in the finale: "Irasshai!" |

Recurring: **Grandpa's umbrella**, a kasa-obake that turns 100 and wakes up (Ep. 3) and now sticks its tongue
out at people; the **zashiki-warashi**, a child spirit who moved in during the storm (Ep. 6) and brings the
house luck; the **maneki-neko** and the **wall of shikishi**, which fills up across the season.

## Format

- **Episodes**: 4-7 minutes, 1920×1080 at 30 fps. A 10-second cold open, the title, chapters per visitor,
  and a 14-second "next time" card for the end screen.
- **Shorts**: 24-56 seconds, 1080×1920, one visitor each, cut from that visitor's episode. They start on a line
  (never a fade), show a hook for the first 2.6 seconds, follow whoever is talking, and loop.
- **Thumbnails**: A = the visitor in the doorway with two lines of hook words; B = the whole shop, no words.
- **Music**: four original tracks composed in code (see README).
