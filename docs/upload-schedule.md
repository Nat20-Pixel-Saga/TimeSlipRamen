  Source: Wikipedia: Oku no Hosomichi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oku_no_Hosomichi

· The Old Pond · 1686: Furuike ya / kawazu tobikomu / mizu no oto: "an old pond / a frog jumps in / the sound of water." Bashō wrote it in 1686. It may be the best-known haiku of all.
  Source: Bashō Memorial Museum, Iga: chronology, https://www.basho-bp.jp/en/?p=10
· Kappa · the dish on its head: A kappa keeps water in a dish on top of its head. If the water spills, it loses its strength until the dish is refilled. Kappa are so polite that if you bow, they bow back... and spill it.
  Source: World History Encyclopedia: Kappa, https://www.worldhistory.org/Kappa/
· Kappamaki: Kappa are famous for loving cucumbers. That's the usual reason given for calling a cucumber sushi roll kappamaki, though nobody knows exactly when the name stuck.
  Source: World History Encyclopedia: Kappa, https://www.worldhistory.org/Kappa/
  Source: Sushi-pedia: kappa, https://www.sushi-pedia.com/en/glossary/kappa

Words on screen
· 暖簾 noren: The cloth curtain over a shop door. Out means open; taken in means closed.
· いらっしゃいませ irasshaimase: "Welcome!" Called out to everyone who walks into a shop.
· ただいま tadaima: "I'm home." Said as you walk back in.
· おかえり okaeri: "Welcome back." Said to someone coming home.
· いただきます itadakimasu: Said before eating: "I humbly receive."
· 模試 moshi: A mock exam (short for mogi shiken), graded A to E.
· お邪魔します ojama shimasu: "Sorry to intrude." Said when entering someone else's place.
· 蕎麦 soba: Thin buckwheat noodles, eaten in Japan for centuries.
· 色紙 shikishi: A stiff square board for poems, pictures and autographs.
· いってらっしゃい itterasshai: "Take care!" Said to someone heading out who will come back.
· 冷やし中華 hiyashi chūka: Cold ramen noodles with toppings, usually a summer dish.
